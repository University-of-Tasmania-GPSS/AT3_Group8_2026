# Results

## CVI-informed washout susceptibility — Model 7

```{figure} figures/Long_Beach_Model_7_CVI_Informed.png
:alt: Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility, Model 7, informed by the CVI erosion layer
:width: 100%

Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility - CVI-informed erosion layer (Model 7).
```

Model 7 produced relatively localised areas of elevated susceptibility, particularly along portions of 
the northern coastline and around the northeastern coastal bend. 
Across the common modelled area, 13.86% of cells were classified as High or Very High 
susceptibility. 
This suggests that higher values in the conventional CVI were concentrated in selected sections of 
the coastal corridor rather than distributed uniformly along the shoreline.

&nbsp;


## Fuzzy-informed washout susceptibility — Model 8

```{figure} figures/Long_Beach_Model_8_Fuzzy_Informed.png
:alt: Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility with the fuzzy overlay-informed erosion layer
:width: 100%

Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility - Fuzzy gamma overlay-informed erosion layer (Model 8).
```

Model 8 produced a more extensive distribution of elevated susceptibility, particularly along the 
northern and eastern coastlines. 
The model classified 28.75% of the common study area as High or Very High susceptibility, 
compared with 13.86% for Model 7. 
Both models were evaluated over 8,808 shared 5 m raster cells, representing 91.73% of the defined 
coastal corridor. 
The larger area identified by Model 8 indicates that the selected coastal vulnerability methodology 
substantially influenced the final susceptibility classifications. 
However, the greater extent of elevated susceptibility does not establish that Model 8 is more 
accurate.

&nbsp;


## Comparison of Models 7 and 8

```{figure} figures/Long_Beach_Model_7_vs_8_Change.png
:alt: Difference in susceptibility between Model 7 and Model 8 at Long Beach
:width: 100%

Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility - change between Model 7 and Model 8.
```

A cell-by-cell comparison was undertaken to identify changes in susceptibility classification 
between the CVI-informed and fuzzy-informed models. 
The results demonstrated that: 

• 47.63% of shared cells increased in susceptibility classification. 

• 52.08% remained unchanged. 

• 0.30% decreased in classification. 

Most changes involved an increase of one susceptibility class, with 4,176 cells increasing by one 
class and only 19 cells increasing by two classes. 
These findings demonstrate the sensitivity of the susceptibility results to the method used to 
characterise coastal vulnerability

## Agreement between the Original Six Models

```{figure} figures/Long_Beach_high_susceptibility_Model_Agreement.png
:alt: Areas classed as highly susceptible by both models at Long Beach
:width: 100%

Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility - high susceptibility model agreement (Models 7 and 8).
```

To reduce reliance on an individual modelling approach, the original six susceptibility models were 
compared to identify areas where multiple scenarios produced similar results. 
Each raster cell was assessed according to the number of models assigning it a High or Very High 
susceptibility classification. 
A total of 1,340 cells were classified as High or Very High by at least four of the six models. 
At a 5 m cell resolution, this represented approximately 33,500 m² of candidate investigation area. 
This comparison was conducted before integrating the two coastal vulnerability outputs and 
therefore does not include Models 7 and 8.

The original six susceptibility models produced different spatial distributions of elevated 
susceptibility, demonstrating the influence of criterion selection and weighting. 
Of the models, the drainage dominant model retuned a classified 9.27% of analysed cells as high 
or very high susceptibility, the smallest of the six models. Suggesting drainage related conditions 
identified relatively locaslied areas of concern rather than continuously elevated susceptibility 
across the entire coastal corridor. 
The coastal exposure model classified 22.05% of cells as high or very high, being the largest of 
the 6 models. Indictatung that coastal exposure criteria identified more extensive areas of 
potential susceptibility along the shoreline. 
AHP-sighted and Fuzzy WLC models had similar classified % with AHP having 19.96% and Fuzzy 
19.62% of high or very high cells. Although these percentages are similar, spatial patterns were as 
identical. Reinforcing the importance of comparing locations Identified by different modelling 
approaches rather than relying on percentages.

## Priority Investigation Zones

```{figure} figures/Priority_washout_investigation_zones.png
:alt: Priority zones for washout investigation at Long Beach
:width: 100%

Long Beach sinkhole susceptibility - priority washout investigation zones.
```

A model agreement analysis was undertaken using the six original susceptibility classifications. 
Each raster cell was assigned an agreement score corresponding to the number of models that 
classified it as high or very high. Cells classified as high or very high by at least four of the six 
models, were then selected as candidate areas for further investigation. 1,340 cells were identified 
releasing 33,500 meters of the coastal corridor. These cells were then processed into 21 priority 
investigation polygons with individual areas of At least 100 meters. The agreement map helps 
highlight locations where elevated susceptibility persists under serval different modelling 
assumptions.
