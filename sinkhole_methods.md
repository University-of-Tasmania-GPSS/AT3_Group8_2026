# Methodology

## Method Description 
The initial analysis was broken down into three primary criteria: proximity to the coastline, terrain 
slope and D8 flow accumulation. Additional criteria low elevation, erosions hazards and soil 
erodibility were introduced to represent different environmental conditions associated with coastal 
washout. Terrain analysis was undertaken using a 5 m digital elevation model and was used to 
calculate flow direction and accumulation, showing potential surface runoff locations. The D8 was 
used to represent modeled surface drainage and does not simulate subsurface water movement 
behind the sea wall. 
To rigorously evaluate potential coastal washout susceptibility at Long Beach, six initial models 
were developed using GIS-based Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA), opposed to relying on 
a single combination of environmental factors. These models were each weighted differently and 
made up of different of environmental factors as seen in figure 1), to investigate how variation in 
criteria and weighting influenced the spatial distribution of susceptibility. 
The six models developed
1. Drainage-Dominant WLC: Increased the importance of surface drainage and low-lying 
terrain.
2. Coastal Exposure WLC: Emphasised mapped coastal erosion hazards and coastline 
proximity.
3. Materials and Erosion WLC: Focused on soil erodibility alongside erosion and drainage 
conditions.
4. AHP-Weighted WLC: Used the Analytic Hierarchy Process to establish criterion weights 
through pairwise comparisons.
5. Fuzzy WLC: Applied fuzzy standardisation to represent more gradual transitions between 
environmental susceptibility levels.
6. Baseline WLC: Combined the initial environmental criteria into an overall susceptibility 
assessment

## Weighted Linear Combination and AHP
Multiple susceptibility scenarios were developed using Weighted Linear Combination (WLC). This 
approach combines standardised environmental criteria according to their relative importance. 
The general WLC equation is: 

$$
S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i x_i
$$ (eq-susceptibility)

Where $S$ represents the relative susceptibility index, $w_i$ is the weight assigned to each criterion, and $x_i$ represents its standardised value.
The initial scenarios considered coastline proximity, flow accumulation, terrain slope, coastal 
erosion hazard and mapped soil erodibility. 
One model incorporated the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), which uses pairwise comparisons 
to determine criterion weights. The AHP analysis produced a consistency ratio of 0.0038, indicating 
strong internal consistency within the weighting comparisons. 

The AHP-weighted model identified variation in susceptibility along the coastal corridor. Higher 
relative values were concentrated in selected sections of the shoreline where contributing 
environmental conditions overlapped. 
These classifications indicate relative susceptibility rather than confirmed areas of ground 
instability. 

## Integrated Coastal Vulnerability Modelling
Following the development of the initial six washout models, two additional scenarios were 
produced using coastal vulnerability results generated by another group member. 
Model 7 incorporated the conventional CVI, while Model 8 incorporated the Fuzzy Gamma 
Overlay result. 
Both datasets were originally produced at 10 m resolution and were aligned to the existing 5 m 
analysis grid. This enabled direct spatial comparisons without increasing the actual detail of the 
original 10 m inputs. 
The coastal vulnerability indices were standardised using their 5th and 95th percentile values within 
the common coastal analysis area. 
Both new models used identical weighting structures: 

| Factor | Weighting |
|:-------|----------:|
| Coastal vulnerability | 50% |
| Flow accumulation | 30% |
| Soil erodibility | 20% |

The only difference between Models 7 and 8 was the coastal vulnerability dataset used. 
These weights were selected as exploratory modelling assumptions rather than calibrated 
probabilities of ground collapse.
