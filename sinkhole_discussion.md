The results demonstrate that susceptibility patterns can vary substantially depending on the 
environmental criteria, weighting assumptions and modelling techniques selected. 
The incorporation of coastal vulnerability modelling produced particularly noticeable differences. 
Although both Models 7 and 8 used identical WLC weights, their classifications differed across 
almost half of their shared cells. 
This highlights the importance of comparing alternative modelling approaches rather than relying 
exclusively on a single susceptibility map. 
The analysis also has several limitations. It relies on available environmental datasets rather than 
direct measurements of seawall condition, subsurface voids or engineered backfill characteristics. 
Furthermore, the flow accumulation model represents surface drainage pathways and cannot 
directly simulate seawater ingress through damaged seawalls. 
The relationship between mapped coastline proximity, coastal erosion and the composite 
vulnerability indices also introduces possible correlation between model inputs. Some available 
datasets have incomplete spatial coverage and differing native resolutions. 
The observed collapse near The Beacon provides an important case study, but a single documented 
occurrence is insufficient to validate the predictive performance of the models. 
Future investigations could incorporate detailed seawall condition surveys, additional records of 
coastal failures, ground-penetrating radar and geotechnical investigations to test for subsurface 
material loss.
