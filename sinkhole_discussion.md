# Discussion

The results demonstrate that susceptibility patterns can vary substantially depending on the 
environmental criteria, weighting assumptions and modelling techniques selected. The difference 
between the six original susceptibility models demonstrates the influence of criterion selection and 
weighting on the final result. The Coastal exposure model identified high percentage of high and 
very high susceptibility 22.05% while drainage dominant model had the small 9.27%. Suggesting 
coastal exposure factors contributed more to mapped susceptibility patterns. The incorporation of 
coastal vulnerability modelling produced particularly noticeable differences. Although both Models 
7 and 8 used identical WLC weights, their classifications differed across almost half of their shared cells. This highlights the importance of comparing alternative modelling approaches rather than relying exclusively on a single susceptibility map. 


Further investigations should focus on validating identified priority zones through field 
observations and targeted subsurface surveys. Ground penetrating radar (GPR) can assist in 
identifying subsurface anomalies potentially associated with voids, disturbed backfill or moisture 
variation. These interpretations would require confirmation through a geotechnical or geophysicist.
